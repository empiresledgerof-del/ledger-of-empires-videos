# Money Lessons from History: strategy and timetable (draft v1, 19 Nov 2026)

Channel name: **Ledger of Empires** (created 19 Nov 2026). Faceless, English, educational only.
Every video tells a TRUE story from history and ends with one clear money or life lesson for today.
Never financial advice, never "buy this". Every description contains "For educational purposes only."

## Goal and the real target
- YPP from **1 Feb 2027** (new applicants): 1,000 subscribers + **8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days**
  (or 20M Shorts views in 90 days). A channel starting in late Nov 2026 cannot reach the old 4,000 hours before
  31 Jan 2027, so 8,000 hours is our real target. Existing YPP members are not affected.
- Lower tier (fan funding, Shopping) stays at 500 subscribers + 3,000 watch hours.
- Math: a 9-minute video at ~40% average viewed = ~3.6 min per view, so 8,000 h needs ~133,000 long-video views
  in a year: about 1,300 views per long video at 2 per week. Shorts views do not count toward watch hours, but
  Metricool's 2026 study found channels that post ~5 Shorts a month get ~46% more views per long video.
  Shorts are the trailer; long videos are the business.

## Audience
- 18-40, curious about money, history and self-improvement. Core: US, UK, Canada, Australia (high ad rates),
  plus India, Pakistan, Philippines. Write for a smart 16-year-old: plain words, short sentences, no jargon
  without a one-line explanation.
- What they want: a gripping true story + "what does this mean for MY money/life today".

## Title and packaging rules
- Outlier titles usually have 2 of 3: a **number**, a **named thing** (Medici, Rockefeller, tulips, Rome),
  a **claim against expectation**. Studies found titles of 30 characters or less got ~60% more views; we aim for
  <= 50 chars and put the main keyword in the first 3 words.
- Examples: "Tulip Mania: When Flowers Cost a House", "Rockefeller's $1 Rule That Built an Empire",
  "Rome Printed Money. Then Rome Fell." (the claim must be TRUE to the video).
- Thumbnails: see channel/thumbnail_playbook.md (Phase 4). One focal subject, 3-4 word hook, huge text.

## How the long-video algorithm decides reach
- Long-form and Shorts are ranked by separate systems; weak Shorts do not hurt long videos.
- Gate 1, **CTR** (impressions click-through): half of all channels sit between 2% and 10%; 4-6% normal, 7%+ good.
  Title + thumbnail decide this.
- Gate 2, **average view duration / % viewed**: the strongest long-form signal. Goal: 50%+ for 6-10 min videos
  (40% is acceptable at the start). A high CTR with low retention hurts: YouTube says low-AVD videos are less likely
  to be recommended.
- Then **satisfaction**: post-view surveys, likes, "Not interested", shares, viewers returning, and whether people
  keep watching YouTube after our video (session).
Rules that follow:
1. **15-second cold open**: start inside the most dramatic moment ("In February 1637, one tulip bulb cost more than
   a house in Amsterdam. Six days later it was worth almost nothing."), then the promise ("and the same trap is
   still taking people's money today"). No logo, no "welcome back".
2. Length **8-10 min** (8+ min allows mid-roll ads). Never pad: cut any sentence that does not move the story.
3. Structure: cold open -> 4-6 chapters (title cards) -> each chapter ends with an open question that pulls into the
   next -> lesson chapter ("What this means for you") -> 20 s outro pointing to one related video.
4. Pattern change every 2-5 s (new shot, zoom, map, stat count-up). A "re-hook" line at ~2-3 min where most people leave.
5. YouTube chapters in the description (0:00 first), main keyword in the first description line.

## How the Shorts algorithm decides reach
- Test audience first; reach widens only if viewers watch instead of swiping.
- Signal 1: **viewed vs swiped away** (first 1-2 s). Aim for 70%+ viewed; ~50-60% is where struggling channels sit.
- Signal 2: **average percentage viewed**: 70%+ healthy; over 100% means re-watches. Then likes, shares, comments,
  surveys, subscribes.
Rules: 25-35 s, the shocking fact in the first sentence AND on screen at frame 1, word-by-word captions, cut every
2-3 s, ending that loops back into the start or asks a question. Each Short links to the related long video.

## Staying safe (inauthentic content policy)
YouTube terminated 16 big AI channels (35M subs, 4.7B views) in Jan 2026 and evaluates whole channels.
Risk signals: template clones (same video, new title), "AI slideshows" (images moving with no real narration or
editing), robotic narration reading Wikipedia, mass uploads. So:
- Every video is a different story with its own structure, hook style, footage and lesson; real editing.
- Script with opinion and insight, not a list of facts. Facts verified against 2+ reliable sources.
- Max 5 uploads per week. Public-domain images only with real movement + narration around them, mixed with footage.

## Lessons from top creators (MrBeast handbook, Paddy Galloway, YouTube's own advice) - added 19 Nov 2026
1. **Packaging first.** For every long video, write 10+ title ideas and 3 thumbnail concepts BEFORE the script.
   If no title/thumbnail pair is exciting, drop the topic. The script is then written to deliver that promise.
2. **The first 5 seconds must show what the thumbnail promised** (same image/subject), then deliver it.
3. **Win a sub-niche first.** Videos 1-8: only two pillars, "Bubbles and crashes" and "Great fortunes", in two
   recognisable repeatable series ("The Rise and Fall of ...", "How ... Made a Fortune"). Expand to the other
   pillars only after the data shows what works. 1 in 3 videos may be an experiment (new format or topic).
4. **Script mechanics:** 2-3 open loops per video (a question raised early, answered later), a re-hook every
   60-90 s, the payoff before the lesson, never a slow middle. Cut anything boring.
5. **Session time:** playlists per series/pillar (each video in 2-3 playlists), and a scripted hand-off in the
   last 20 s that names ONE specific next video, matching the single end-screen video.
6. **Shorts feed long videos:** most Shorts are the best 25-35 s moment of a long video, ending on an open
   question, with the Short's "Related video" set to that long video.
7. **Prediction sheet:** before each upload, record predicted 7-day views in performance.csv. After 7 days,
   label the miss as idea, packaging (CTR) or content (retention) and write it in learnings.md.
8. **Native A/B testing of titles/thumbnails** (Studio "A/B Testing", up to 3 versions, winner chosen by
   watch-time share) is only for YPP members. Until then we pick the best of 3 thumbnails ourselves; switch to the
   native test the day the channel joins YPP.
9. **Channel page:** pin the best long video as the featured video for new visitors and add playlist sections.
